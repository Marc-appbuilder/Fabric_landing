The reality

You've heard it.

In the minibus. At the pool table. In the group chat. Youth workers are often the first adults to hear it, and the ones young people trust enough to say it to.

Underneath there's usually something real: no decent work, nowhere to live, nowhere to belong. Far-right content offers someone to blame, and more and more that someone is women, feminism, LGBTQI+ people and migrants.

Myth-busting tends to shut the conversation down. A referral can feel like the only other option. This training gives you a third one.

The training

Not another myth-busting session.

Practical methods from community organising, built for real youth work. You'll learn to:

Read the gender lens

See how far-right and anti-feminist narratives use masculinity, status and grievance to draw in young men, and how they affect young women, girls and LGBTQI+ young people.

Hold difficult conversations

Listen beneath the narrative with Socratic questioning, and keep the conversation open instead of shutting it down.

Build a story of us

Replace blame with a shared story built on relationships, common interests and the power to change things together.

Grow belonging

Create spaces where young people feel connected, valued and heard. Belonging is the strongest protection against division.

Strengthen identity

Support positive, healthy identities, including positive masculinity, that don't depend on putting others down.

Use preventative digital tools

Get to know our Community Builder platform for mapping relationships, spotting young leaders and organising community action.
